## ALSCL 2.5.1

- The default plot style and its palette are now named "default": `acl_theme_set(style = "default")` and `acl_theme_set(palette = "default")`. ALSCL 2.5.0 named them "standard"; that name is still accepted and gives a warning once per session. Figures and all other settings are unchanged.

## ALSCL 2.5.0

### Default look of the figures

- The default plot style changed, so figures made with the default settings look different from those of 2.4.5. Estimates, standard errors and saved fits are unchanged. Spawning stock biomass, total biomass and abundance are teal lines with a shaded 95% band, fishing mortality is an orange line with a band, total recruitment is drawn as light-blue bars and total catch as dark-blue bars, both with thin dark-grey error bars with a light outline when `se = TRUE`, and panels at age or at length are lines in the color of their quantity. Lines are thinner: the default `line_size` is 1 and `compare_line_size` 0.9, in place of 1.5 and 1.2. The y axes of these plots, and of `plot_compare_ts()` and `plot_compare_F_ts()`, start at zero. Titles are black, bold and left aligned, facet labels are bold text without a box, legends are below the plot and, as before, no grid lines are drawn. The other plots draw estimates in dark blue and observations in orange, the two fits of a comparison are dark blue and orange, heat maps run from white to dark blue, and in `plot_retro()` the full fit is orange and the earlier fits are grey.
- `acl_theme_set(style = "classic")` brings back the look of 2.4.5 in one call: the npg palette, lines for every series, the line widths 1.5 and 1.2, centered plain titles, facet labels in grey boxes, y axes that follow the data and the legend position of each plot. It does not bring back the former texts inside the figures, the former number of facet columns or the former tick labels, because the rules for texts, time axes and layout below apply under both styles. `acl_theme_set(style = "standard")` or `acl_theme_reset()` returns to the new default.
- Each feature of a style is also a setting of its own, so one feature can be changed and the others kept: `acl_theme_set(bars = FALSE)` draws total recruitment and total catch as lines, `acl_theme_set(y_zero = FALSE)` lets the y axes follow the data, `acl_theme_set(palette = "npg")` brings back the npg colors of lines, observations, comparisons and heat maps, `acl_theme_set(title_color = "quantity")` gives the titles of the stock size, fishing mortality, recruitment and catch plots the color of their quantity, and `acl_theme_set(grid = "y")` adds horizontal grid lines. The new settings are `style`, `grid`, `title_face`, `title_color`, `show_title`, `stock_color`, `F_color`, `recruitment_color`, `catch_color`, `bars`, `y_zero`, `strip`, `legend_position`, `axis_color` and `x_angle`. `palette` accepts `"standard"`, the palette of the default style, next to the five ggsci palettes, and a style also sets `title_hjust`, `line_size`, `compare_line_size`, `se_alpha` and `compare_se_alpha`. The function reference lists the value of every setting under both styles.

### Titles, texts and time axes

- Every plot function takes `title`: `NULL` draws the default title, `FALSE` (also `NA` or `""`) draws no title, and a character string is drawn as given. The eight comparison plots `plot_compare_ts()`, `plot_compare_F()`, `plot_compare_F_ts()`, `plot_compare_CatL()`, `plot_compare_growth()`, `plot_compare_selectivity()`, `plot_compare_residuals()` and `plot_compare_metrics()` have this argument now. `acl_theme_set(show_title = FALSE)` removes the default titles of all plots and keeps the titles given in a call and the titles that name the panels of a multi-panel figure. The titles of the three `check_fit()` panels are now keys of `titles` (`check_residuals`, `check_lag` and `check_qq`), so they can be changed or removed.
- The default texts inside the figures changed: titles, axis titles, facet labels, legends and panel texts use the full names of the quantities, without code names or undefined abbreviations. For example "Estimated spawning stock biomass (SSB) with 95% CI" is now "Spawning stock biomass with 95% confidence intervals", the y axis "Catch numbers" is "Catch in numbers", and the title "{m1} vs {m2}" of `plot_compare_ts()` is "Time series of {m1} and {m2}". The panels of `plot_retro()` are named "Total biomass", "Recruitment", "Spawning stock biomass (SSB)" and "Total abundance", with the y axis "Estimate" and the text "Mohn's rho = ..."; `plot_self_test()` names the parameters in words, such as "Asymptotic length (Linf)" for `log_Linf`; and `plot_CatL()` has a legend for the observed and fitted series. Texts set with `acl_theme_set(titles = , xlab = , ylab = )` or with the arguments of a plot are used unchanged.
- Time axes follow one rule in all plots. A single panel labels every year up to 12 years, every second year up to 30, every fifth year up to 60 and every tenth year beyond, with unlabelled minor ticks at the other years or time steps. In a figure with several panels the label step follows the width of a panel, and labels that would touch are slanted by 45 degrees instead of being dropped. The new setting `x_angle`, also an argument of `theme_acl()`, sets the angle: `NULL` chooses it, `0` keeps the labels horizontal and thins them, and another number fixes the angle. `x_breaks` in a call or in the settings still wins.

### Layout and saved figures

- The default number of facet columns follows the number of panels, for example 4 panels as 2 x 2, 15 as 5 x 3, 20 as 5 x 4 and 23 as 6 x 4; `facet_ncol` and `facet_nrow` in a call win. The defaults of the setting `compare_facet_ncol` and of `plot_compare_CatL(facet_ncol = )` changed from 2 and 4 to `NULL`, which chooses the columns in the same way; set them to keep the former layout.
- `plot_compare_ts()` with five quantities and `plot_compare_metrics()` put the legend in the empty cell of the grid. `plot_SSB_Rec()` and the quantile-quantile panel of `check_fit()` are square. Reference lines at zero and one and lines of equality are thin and drawn under the data. Unless `line_size` is given, lines are thinner in figures with many panels: full width up to 4 panels, two thirds for 5 to 12 panels and half beyond. Tick labels are thinned by the number of panel rows, drop trailing zeros and do not overlap, text inside the plots is at least 8 pt, also when a smaller `text_size` of `plot_VB()` or `label_size` of `plot_compare_metrics()` is given, and long legends wrap. `plot_compare_metrics()` draws horizontal bars and writes the value of each bar beside it without rotating it, with room for these values, and `plot_retro()` leaves room in each panel for the Mohn's rho text. Plots that draw points keep about a point radius between a point and the panel border on the x axis, and in short facet panels on the y axis too, so that no point is cut by the border, unless `x_expand` or the expansion of the plot itself is set. Caps of error bars on points or lines are a quarter of the spacing of the x values and stay short when a plot has a single time step; caps on bars are a third of the bar width. The defaults of `se_width` in `plot_deviance()` and `errorbar_width` in `plot_compare_residuals()` changed from 0.5 and 0.3 to `NULL`, which chooses this width; a value given in the call is used as before. The default size of the growth annotation of `plot_VB()`, `text_size`, is 3.5 in place of 5.
- New functions `acl_figure_size()` and `acl_save_plot()`. `acl_figure_size(plot)` returns the recommended width and height in inches for saving a plot, computed from its layout: about 8 x 4.5 inches for a single time series and 10 inches wide for figures with panels; `width =` fixes the width and returns the matching height. `acl_save_plot(plot, filename)` saves a plot at that size, on a white background and at 300 dpi by default. The figures written by `output = TRUE` are saved with it at the recommended size of each plot, instead of 12 x 7 inches, and the code in the documentation saves figures at 300 dpi.

### Theme helpers and new arguments

- New functions `theme_acl()` and `acl_palette()`, so ALSCL now exports 81 functions. `theme_acl()` returns the theme layers of the package plots under the current settings (base theme, text sizes, grid lines, title, facet labels and legend position), so that `ggplot(...) + theme_acl()` gives a figure of your own the look of the package plots. `acl_palette()` returns the colors of the active palette or of a named one, `n` of them when `n` is given, and with `role` the colors that the plots use for stock size, F, recruitment, catch, lines, observations, smoothers and reference lines.
- `plot_recruitment()` and `plot_catch()` have a new argument `geom`: `"bar"` or `"line"` for one call, while the default `NULL` follows the setting `bars`. Catch at age and at length stay lines. The default of `se_type` in `plot_recruitment()` is now `NULL`, which draws error bars on bars and a ribbon around a line; 2.4.5 drew a ribbon. The default of `palette` in `plot_ridges()` is now `NULL`, which gives a ramp from light to dark blue under the standard palette and, as before, viridis under any other palette; `palette = "viridis"` gives the former ramp under either style.

### Intervals and messages

- `extract_estimates()` and `summarize_simulations()` have a new argument `interval`, and its default is the lognormal interval `exp(log(estimate) -/+ z * SE / estimate)`, the interval that the plots and `self_test()` already used. Tables, coverage and figures therefore show the same interval, and the interval of a positive quantity stays above zero. By default the `conf_low` and `conf_high` columns of `extract_estimates()` and the coverage of `summarize_simulations()` differ from those of 2.4.5; `interval = "wald"` gives the former bounds `estimate -/+ z * SE`. An estimate of zero keeps the Wald bounds with the lower bound stopped at zero, and a negative estimate keeps the Wald bounds. Estimates and standard errors are unchanged. `self_test()` now builds its intervals with the same code; its results for positive estimates are unchanged.
- The message of an ALSCL fit about the t0 that a supplied `log_t0` means (`t0 = t0_lower + exp(log_t0)`) is now given only when `t0_lower` and `log_t0` come from different places: one from the arguments of the call and the other from the input bundle or the `recipe` of a saved fit. `run_alscl(YTF_example, t0_lower = -1)` still gives it, because the bundle stores `log_t0 = log(1/60)`, chosen for `t0_lower = 0`, which then means a t0 of -0.983 years. A bundle that stores both, such as `whiting_example`, and a call that gives both no longer give the message. The message for a positive `t0_lower` that is not below the default t0 is unchanged.

## ALSCL 2.4.5

- `self_test()` keeps the estimate of the source fit as the truth of mean_log_F and mean_log_R in conditional mode, as in 2.4.4, and its help and the model validation article now explain why. At the estimate, the mean parameter of an AR(1) process equals a weighted mean of the fitted process in which the first and last time steps weigh more, not its arithmetic mean, and a refit that recovers the process recovers this weighted mean. A development version after 2.4.4 used the source estimate plus the arithmetic mean of the fitted deviations; that truth compared unlike quantities and reversed the sign of the bias. In a self-test with 20 data sets (YTF_example, ALSCL) the median relative error of mean_log_F was -4.2% against that truth, +12.7% against the source estimate and +7.1% when the arithmetic means of the fitted and refitted log F are compared. The level of F and recruitment is measured by the time series.
- With `structure = list(initial_deviations = "independent")` the recruitment reported for the first time step (`Rec` and its standard error, and so `plot_recruitment()`, `plot_SSB_Rec()`, `extract_estimates()` and the truth of `self_test()`) is the number of the first age class in that step, which includes its initial deviation; it omitted that deviation. The estimates and fits with the default form are unchanged.
- `run_acl()` and `run_alscl()` give a message when `survey_q_age` changes the default start of mean_log_R by `-log(survey_q_age[1])`, and say how to supply a start. The message is given once per call and not with `silent = TRUE`.
- `compare_models()$growth` has been a table with the columns Model, Parameter, Estimate and Meaning since 2.4.4. Code written for the earlier table, such as `cmp$growth$ALSCL`, now stops with an error that shows `subset(cmp$growth, Model == "ALSCL")`, instead of returning NULL. Printing and `write.csv()` are unchanged.
- `plot_VB(fit, se = TRUE)` for an ALSCL fit still draws no interval. Its warning now says how to draw the interval of the figure before 2.4.4: scripts that relied on that interval must add `parameter_curve = TRUE`, which draws the curve computed from Linf, k and t0 with its interval.
- References: the help of `self_test()` lists all authors of Deroba et al. (2015). The help of `survey_catchability()` cites Thompson (1994) only for the confounding of a selectivity that declines at older ages with the natural mortality rate, and Cook (2013) for the survey index alone. The help of `whiting_example` states that `ices$M` and `ices$maturity` equal Tables 22.15 and 22.14 of the WGNSSK 2026 report in every cell.
- `run_alscl()` with `t0_lower` and no supplied start of `log_t0`: the default start is converted so that t0 keeps its default value of 1/60 year, as default correlation starts keep theirs under the symmetric range. It was read as `t0_lower + 1/60`, so with `t0_lower = -1` the fit fixed t0 at -0.98 years without a message. Fits that supply `log_t0`, and fits with the default `t0_lower = 0`, are unchanged.
- `self_test()` divides the error by the absolute value of the truth, so a positive relative error is an overestimate also for a negative truth, such as a negative t0 or correlation. Relative errors of positive truths are unchanged.
- `hindcast_model()` accepts negative fitted correlations of recruitment and F, which `structure = list(correlation_range = "symmetric")` allows; such folds were reported as failed predictions. `profile_model()` drops map entries of an archive saved by 2.4.3 that release a parameter the model does not have, as `refit_model()` and `summary()` do; every grid fit of such an archive failed.
- `project_model()` and `simulate_fit()` reject an older fit without stored diagnostics when one of its stored fields shows a failure (a nonzero convergence code, a gradient of 0.001 or more, a Hessian that is not positive definite or a bound reached); such fits were used with status "unknown". `allow_failed_fit = TRUE` still allows them.
- Whole-number time steps of a quarterly fit are labelled as first quarters ("2004 Q1"), not "Year 2004", in the legend of `plot_retro()` (for example with `peel_step = 4` when the data end in a first quarter) and in the subtitles of `plot_relative_catch()`, `plot_VB()` and `plot_length_at_age()`.
- `plot_self_test()`: with `what = "both"` the figure title follows `font_family`, `title_size` and `title_hjust`, and the new argument `facet_scales` (default `"free_y"`) sets the scales of the time-series panels.
- Failure records of batch fits (class `alscl_fit_error`) have `logLik()`, `nobs()` and `fitted()` methods: `AIC()` and `BIC()` give NA, so a loop over a batch no longer stops at a failed fit. The plots, `extract_estimates()`, `extract_parameters()`, `refit_model()`, `project_model()` and `simulate_fit()` stop on a failure record with its error message, instead of a message about missing metadata.
- `run_acl()`, `run_alscl()`, `retro_model()` and the batch functions give a message when `structure = list(correlation_range = "symmetric")` reads a supplied correlation start on the symmetric scale. For example the `logit_log_R` start of `YTF_example`, a correlation of 0.1 under the default range, becomes a fixed correlation of -0.8; the message names the value and says how to keep it. Not with `silent = TRUE`.
- An ALSCL fit with `t0_lower` different from 0 gives a message when it reads a supplied `log_t0`, with the t0 that the value means. For example `YTF_example` fixes `log_t0 = log(1/60)`, which becomes a t0 of -0.983 years with `t0_lower = -1`. A positive `t0_lower` that is not below the default t0 of 1/60 year also gives a message. `run_acl()`, `run_alscl()`, `refit_model()`, `retro_model()`, `hindcast_model()` and `fit_scenarios()` give a message when a `parameters` list in the call leaves out stored parameter values: the list replaces the stored one, and the message names the values that are no longer used and those that the map still fixes at their defaults. For example `run_alscl(YTF_example, parameters = list(logit_log_R = qlogis(0.2)))` fixes Linf at 55, the default, in place of the stored 60. The note on correlation starts under the symmetric range now says to set the value in the parameters list that the fit uses. Not with `silent = TRUE`.
- `sim_model()` and `sim_alscl()` take `t0_lower`, so replicates generated with a negative t0 can be fitted with the true growth. `simulate_example_data(seed = )` uses the random-number generator of `sim_data()`, so a seed gives the same data in any session; with the default generator the data are unchanged. Restoring a session that uses `RNGversion("3.5.0")` after `sim_data()`, `simulate_fit()` or `self_test()` no longer repeats R's warning about the "Rounding" sampler.
- When every start of a fit fails, the error says whether TMB was built with another Matrix ABI version than that of the installed Matrix package, and gives the remedy, `install.packages("TMB", type = "source")`. Such a TMB binary can make every start fail. This applies to `run_acl()`, `run_alscl()` and the functions that call them. ALSCL now requires TMB 1.9.21 or later, the oldest version used in its checks; earlier versions were allowed but are not covered by the checks. The installation page and the troubleshooting table describe the warning that TMB gives as it loads. The R-CMD-check workflow also checks the package with the previous R release on Windows, and the installation page says what to do when the available TMB is older than 1.9.21.
- A fit whose covariance matrix is not positive definite no longer gives the R warning "NaNs produced" from the calculation of the standard errors. The standard errors are unchanged, with NaN where a variance is negative. The failed numerical checks name the Hessian as before. An example is the fit in the diagnostics article that releases the correlation of F across ages, `cfg$map["logit_log_F_a"] <- list(NULL)` followed by `run_acl(x, map = cfg$map)`.
- Documentation: the help pages of `run_acl()` and `run_alscl()` are regenerated from their source; they still described the first-period recruitment before the change above. The help of `polish` (the second refinement pass), `t0_lower`, `inspect_parameters()` and `extract_parameters()` (the transforms of the symmetric range and of `t0_lower`) and `simulation_to_tables()` (pass `len_border = sm$len_border` when the length bins have unequal widths) is corrected. The R-CMD-check workflow fails when the help pages differ from the roxygen comments. `citation("ALSCL")` gives the model paper and the package (new `inst/CITATION`), and the package description cites the model paper. The README counts seven worked cases. English text uses hyphens for ranges and compounds, and `website/check.py` rejects en and em dashes on English pages and verifies the plot-function, worked-case and chapter counts quoted in the READMEs.

## ALSCL 2.4.4

- Fixes from a full code review. Default fits keep their estimates; changes that alter results are listed first. Changed results: (1) `sim_model()`, `sim_acl()` and `sim_alscl()` fit each replicate with the natural mortality rate recorded in its file (the default was `M = 0.2`); a supplied `M` that differs is an error unless `allow_M_mismatch = TRUE`. (2) In `retro_model()`, a `mean_log_R` fixed through `map` without a value uses the full-data value in every peel. (3) Interval plots draw lognormal intervals by default (see below). (4) `plot_SSB_Rec()` pairs SSB with recruitment at the youngest fitted age, so quarterly fits with `rec.age = 0.25` use a lag of one quarter, not four, and annual fits with a recruitment age other than 1 use that age. (5) `run_acl()` uses data-based Linf start and bounds when its defaults do not suit the length bins, and for M above 0.7 its default init_Z start is no longer moved onto the upper bound.
- New `structure` argument of `run_acl()`, `run_alscl()` and `retro_model()` selects optional model structures; the default is the original structure, that of the package as first released. `initial_plus_group = "equilibrium"` makes the first-period plus group the sum of all older cohorts at init_Z. For ALSCL, `initial_length = "growth"` builds the first-period lengths at age from the growth transition, `growth_increment = "von_bertalanffy"` uses the von Bertalanffy increment over the time step, and `growth_transition = "bin"` integrates the transition over the source length bin. With `initial_length = "growth"` and `initial_plus_group = "equilibrium"` together, a population with constant recruitment and no fishing stays constant; with the original structure it does not. `sim_cal()` and `sim_data()` take the same list as `params$structure`.
- A map that ties or fixes entries of dev_log_R, dev_log_F or dev_log_N0 is rejected when the matching standard deviation or correlation is estimated, because those estimates were biased (the standard deviation could run to its bound). With these parameters fixed the map is accepted, and the fit lists the arrays in `restricted_random`. Use `F_structure = "year"` for one F deviation per time step.
- The ACL defaults for Linf (start 60, bounds 10 to 100) are replaced by data-based values when they do not suit the length bins, for example tuna lengths in cm: the start is 1.1 times the length below which 99 percent of the summed survey index lies, the upper bound is twice the largest bin midpoint, and the lower bound is not raised above the default. Starting values moved into their bounds are reported (a warning for supplied starts, a message for default starts) and listed in `clamped_starts`. The default init_Z start no longer sits on its upper bound for large M.
- New `start_spread` argument sets the spread of the second and later starts. Fits record `start_summary`; converged starts that differ by more than 2 objective units are added to `diagnostics$issues`. The help pages explain that a single start can stop at a local optimum that passes every numerical check. Sequential multistart fits reuse one TMB object with identical results.
- Fits report the number of zero survey observations treated as missing. Numeric length labels with unequal spacing and no `len_border` give a warning. Logical survey, weight or maturity columns are rejected. `create_parameters()` errors name the unknown parameter and its name in the other model. `read_survey_excel()` stops on Excel error cells (#DIV/0!, #N/A) and names the sheet and cells. `len_lower` and `len_upper` are deprecated in `prepare_alscl_data()`, `retro_model()` and bundles fitted with ACL (they cannot change a fit); `run_alscl()` still accepts them without a warning.
- `sim_data()` and `simulate_example_data(seed = )` no longer change the caller's random-number stream. Replicates record their seed, generator settings and package version, and a replicate ID gives the same data under any session generator. Replicate files are written only after every replicate is valid, with integer names (`sim_rep100000`). A zero AR(1) coefficient no longer warns, `initialize_params()$years` ends at the last observation period, and a discontinuous dome selectivity gives a warning. `simulate_example_data()` reports with `message()`.
- Results of `sim_model()`, `sim_acl()` and `sim_alscl()` no longer hold the TMB object (about 0.9 GiB per ALSCL replicate); use `refit_model()` when one is needed. New `simulation_to_tables()` converts a `sim_data()` replicate to fitting tables. `summarize_simulations()` also returns `summary_converged` and `failures`. Failure records of batch fits have class `alscl_fit_error` with print, summary, coef and vcov methods.
- `print()`, `summary()`, `extract_parameters()` and `refit_model()` work again on assessments saved by 2.4.3. A fit has one numerical-screen verdict in all summaries. `save_assessment(x = )` keeps the observations and time metadata of an older fit; archives use RDS version 3. `vcov()` returns NULL when the covariance is not finite or not positive definite. New `fitted()`, `logLik()` (so `AIC()` and `BIC()`) and `nobs()` methods. `extract_estimates()` has a new last column `extrapolated` for terminal-period F and catch, which no observation informs.
- `retro_model()`: new `peel_step` (for example 4 for annual peels of a quarterly model); per-fit warnings in `fit_diagnostics$warnings`; `retro_acl(x, 3)` is accepted; the full fit runs in the same batch as the peels; delta-method standard errors that the retrospective does not use are skipped (also in `hindcast_model()` without `save_fits`). `plot_retro()` draws fits that failed the numerical screen with dashed lines, keeps fits without estimates in the legend, keeps colours with the peel and labels quarterly peels as "2001 Q4".
- `compare_models()` records `delta_aic`, `aic_threshold` and `reason`, and reports a failed fit as such. A bundle whose `fit_config` has an entry for one model can be fitted with the other; misplaced top-level fitting arguments give a warning. `check_fit()` describes the QQ reference correctly: standardized conditional residuals have a standard deviation below 1 under a correct model.
- Plots: intervals of abundance, biomass, SSB, recruitment, catch and fishing mortality are lognormal by default, `exp(log(estimate) +/- 1.96 * SE / estimate)`, and cannot fall below zero; use `interval = "wald"` or `acl_theme_set(interval = "wald")` for the previous interval. Age panels and axes show fitted ages and mark the plus group ("Age 15+"). `plot_compare_F_ts()` gains `basis = "age"` for a common age basis. A global `x_breaks` applies only to axes it lies on. Faceted survey plots no longer overprint year labels. Error bars are opaque by default. `plot_ridges()` has one figure title and quarter labels. Shared F is drawn as one panel. Labels state the time basis of F and spell out SSB. `acl_theme_set()` rejects unsupported settings.
- Speed and infrastructure: the compile cache key includes the platform and C++ settings and builds are installed atomically (existing persistent caches are rebuilt once). `sd_cores = "auto"` respects `SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK` and `options(mc.cores)` and avoids forking with a threaded BLAS. Sequential batch fits use the session's `sd_cores`. A malformed speed option stops the fit with a message that names it. Diagnostics report unfinished refinement and standard deviations close to zero. Socket workers stop when they load a different TMB version. Internal options `ALSCL.se_level` and `ALSCL.delta_method` are available.
- The development version is 2.4.3.9000. `reshape2` and `cowplot` are no longer required. The built package no longer contains unused figures. Documentation states that outputs are on the scale of the survey index unless the index is absolute, corrects the `growth_step` default and the description of process maps, and maintainer scripts check that the loaded package has the code of the checkout.
- Tests compare the joint likelihood, the growth transition matrix, the population dynamics and Mohn's rho with independent R implementations, check that the operating models reproduce the templates, and fit `YTF_example` to check recovery of the simulated truth.
- `structure` has a fifth setting, `correlation_range`, for ACL and ALSCL. `"positive"` (default) keeps the AR(1) correlations of recruitment and F between 0 and 1, the logistic transform of the `logit_*` parameters. `"symmetric"` lets them lie between -1 and 1 (2 * logistic - 1), so a correlation of zero is no longer a boundary in the numerical checks and a negative correlation can be estimated. Under `"symmetric"` a correlation rho is `qlogis((rho + 1) / 2)` on the optimizer scale; default starts keep their natural value, and `inspect_parameters()`, `extract_parameters()`, `profile_model()` and `simulate_fit()` use the same transform. Use it when recruitment has no or negative autocorrelation: with the default range such a fit runs to the lower boundary and fails the numerical checks.
- `structure` accepts the name of a set: `"original"` or `"von_bertalanffy"`. `"original"` is the default structure: the structure of the package as first released, which implements the model of Zhang and Cadigan (2022). Two details of its growth increment are in the code and not in the paper: the lengths at which the logistic increment is 50% and 95% of its maximum (0.5 Linf and 0.05 Linf) and the multiplication of the increment by the time step. `"von_bertalanffy"` selects `growth_increment = "von_bertalanffy"`, `growth_transition = "bin"`, `initial_length = "growth"` and `initial_plus_group = "equilibrium"` together (for ACL, the plus-group setting). A list can name a set and add settings, for example `list(set = "von_bertalanffy", correlation_range = "symmetric")`. `"published"` and `"consistent"` are accepted as the earlier names of `"original"` and `"von_bertalanffy"`. Use the four settings together: in simulation tests with quarterly time steps and 5 cm length bins, fits with only some of them failed the numerical checks or were strongly biased. `"von_bertalanffy"` is also a value of the single setting `growth_increment`. `structure = list(growth_increment = "von_bertalanffy")` changes one setting, and `structure = "von_bertalanffy"` changes all four. An ALSCL fit whose `structure` list changes one, two or three of the four settings gives a message that names the settings left at their original values (not with `silent = TRUE`; `fit_scenarios()`, `hindcast_model()` and `retro_model()` give it once per call). A list that names the set and replaces one of its settings, for example `list(set = "von_bertalanffy", growth_transition = "midpoint")`, gives no message. `run_alscl()` stores `growth_check`. When growth under `growth_transition = "midpoint"` stops below 90% of the estimated Linf, that is, when fish stay in a length bin with a probability of at least 0.99 per time step, the fit reports it in a message and `summary()` prints it. A fit that would fail the gradient check (largest component 0.001 or more) gets a second pass of refinement steps. This pass accepts a step that lowers the gradient when the objective rises by no more than its working precision, which matters when the likelihood is very sharp in one parameter, such as Linf under the von Bertalanffy increment. Fits that pass the gradient check after the first pass are unchanged.
- New argument `t0_lower` (default 0) of `run_alscl()` and `retro_model()`. Reported t0 values are on the natural scale. In an input bundle, store it under `fit_config$alscl`.

- Fit diagnostics. The numerical screen now fails a fit in which a parameter is at its limit in practice although it has not reached an optimizer bound: `log_t0` below -10 (ALSCL; t0 equals `t0_lower`), the logarithm of a standard deviation below -9 (a variance of zero), or a logit correlation with an absolute value above 15. ## ALSCL 2.1.0

### Speed settings on every system

Standard errors now also use several cores on Windows and in RStudio, R.app and Positron: socket workers compute the delta method when it would take more than about 3 seconds, with identical results. Each fit records how its standard errors were computed in `fit$sd_info`, and `alscl_speed_info()` shows what the settings mean on your computer. The new [speed settings](../articles/speed.html) article explains every setting.

### Speed settings

The new `alscl_speed()` sets how fits compute standard errors. By default they are computed on several cores within one fit, with results identical to ALSCL 2.0.0; in the benchmark, fits were 1.13-1.24 times faster. `alscl_speed(framework = "TMBad")` compiles the models with TMB's TMBad library and made fits 1.25-1.68 times faster, but estimates differ slightly from the default. See the [`alscl_speed()` reference](../reference/alscl_speed.html).

### Parallel fitting update

`run_acl()` and `run_alscl()` now accept `nstarts` separately from `ncores`, with the previous default behavior preserved. Returned diagnostics record actual worker PIDs and CPU time. See [measured parallel performance](../articles/reproducibility.html) for equal-work timings and reproducible evidence.
